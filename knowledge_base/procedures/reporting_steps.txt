General steps for reporting a non-emergency incident:
1. Ensure your immediate safety first. If you are in danger or need medical
   help, contact emergency services before completing any written report.
2. Write down or record what happened as soon as possible while details are
   fresh: what occurred, where, when, and who was involved (if known).
3. Preserve evidence rather than altering it — do not delete messages,
   move damaged items more than necessary, or edit photos before submitting
   them as evidence.
4. Note any witnesses and how to reach them, if they are willing to be
   contacted.
5. Gather supporting evidence: photographs of damage or the scene, screenshots
   of messages, receipts or serial numbers for stolen items, and any relevant
   documents.
6. Provide accurate contact information so the relevant authority can follow
   up with you if needed.
7. Keep a copy of your report and any reference/tracking number you are
   given.

What evidence is generally useful (this varies by incident type):
- Photographs of damage, the scene, or visible injuries
- Screenshots of threatening or fraudulent messages
- Serial numbers, IMEI numbers, or receipts for stolen items
- Names or descriptions of witnesses
- Timestamps: when the incident happened and when it was discovered
- Any video footage (e.g. doorbell camera, dashcam) if available

Emergency vs non-emergency: An emergency is a situation involving immediate
danger to life, an ongoing crime, a medical emergency, or a fire. In an
emergency, contact emergency services immediately by phone rather than
filling out a written report first. A non-emergency report is appropriate
when the incident has already concluded, no one is in immediate danger, and
you are documenting something for the record or for follow-up investigation.

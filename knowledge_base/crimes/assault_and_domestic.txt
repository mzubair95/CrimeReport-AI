Assault refers to an act that causes a person to fear imminent physical harm,
or that causes actual physical harm (sometimes distinguished as "assault" vs
"battery" depending on jurisdiction). If an assault is in progress or someone
is currently injured, this is a medical/emergency priority before any report
is filed. Useful information for an assault report includes: what physical
contact or threat occurred, whether there was any injury and whether medical
attention was sought, whether the person involved is known to the victim, and
whether there were any witnesses.

Domestic incidents involve conflict, threats, or harm between people who share
a household or intimate relationship (partners, family members, etc.).
Domestic situations often carry an elevated safety risk because the person
involved may have ongoing access to the victim. Safety planning (a safe place
to stay, a trusted contact) is often as important as the report itself.
Reports should avoid requiring the victim to specify graphic detail beyond
what is needed to establish what happened, when, and whether there is an
ongoing safety risk.

Harassment and threats cover repeated unwanted contact, intimidation, or
statements that put a person in fear of harm, including in person, by phone,
or online. A useful report captures: the nature and frequency of the contact,
whether explicit threats of violence were made, whether the person is known
to the victim, and any saved evidence such as messages, call logs, or
screenshots.

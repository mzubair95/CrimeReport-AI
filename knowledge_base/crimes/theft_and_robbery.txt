Theft is the taking of another person's property without consent and without
the use of force or threat against a person. Common examples include
pickpocketing, shoplifting, bicycle theft, and theft from a vehicle. A useful
theft report usually includes: what item(s) were taken, an approximate value,
where and when the item was last seen, whether the location was locked or
secured, and any identifying marks or serial numbers on the item (e.g. a
phone's IMEI number, a laptop's serial number).

Robbery differs from theft because it involves the use or threat of force
against a person to take property directly from them. Robbery is generally
treated as a more serious offense than simple theft because of the personal
danger involved. If a robbery just occurred and the suspect may still be
nearby, or if there was a weapon involved or any injury, this is an emergency
situation and immediate contact with emergency services takes priority over
completing a written report. A useful robbery report includes: a description
of what was taken, whether a weapon was seen, a general (non-identifying)
description of the suspect(s) such as approximate number of people, clothing,
and direction they fled, and whether anyone was injured.

Burglary refers to unlawful entry into a building, home, or vehicle with the
intent to commit a crime, typically theft, often when the occupants are not
present. Useful details include how entry appears to have been made (e.g. a
broken window, forced door, unlocked entry), what if anything was taken or
disturbed, and approximately when the property was last known to be secure
versus when the burglary was discovered.
